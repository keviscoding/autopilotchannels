## Philosophy (from teaching — not guru slop)

You don’t need a secret niche. You need a **better way to decide** whether an opportunity is worth entering:

1. **Demographic arbitrage** — older living-room viewers → advertisers pay more (teaching ranges often cited: ~$8–$20 RPM on mature topics vs ~$2 on teen/trend niches).
2. **Low entry barrier** — $20–$30 sofa edit, no face, no film crew.
3. **Not AI slop that gets banned** — repeatable documentary / archive / public-footage formats with compliance where needed.
4. **Emergence** — young channels where views beat subs beat “graveyard” 170k-sub / 16k-view corpses.
5. **Winning format evidence** — more than one upload proving the structure, not a 1-upload fluke.

Kevis’s self-improvement year: monetized, **~$2 RPM**, first payday ~$3, ~$1,000 over six months while thinking 1,000-view drips meant progress. The scorecard exists so you don’t romanticize the wrong niche for a year.

---

## Scoring model

Each criterion: **0 / 1 / 2**  
- **0** = Fail / high risk  
- **1** = Weak / needs proof  
- **2** = Clear pass  

**Max = 24** (12 criteria × 2)

| Total | Verdict | Action |
|-------|---------|--------|
| **20–24** | **PASS** | Plan first 10 videos. Controlled spend. |
| **15–19** | **BORDERLINE** | Fix the weak criteria or park. Do not scale editors yet. |
| **0–14** | **KILL** | Next candidate. Spend $0. |

### Hard gates (automatic KILL even if total is high)

Any of these = **KILL**, ignore the points:

1. Needs a charismatic face / personal brand to work  
2. Requires on-location film crew or owned restoration shop  
3. Teen / challenge / kids / Roblox-Minecraft-anime primary feed neighbors  
4. Clear copyright landmine you cannot license (ripping BBC, full movies, unlicensed music beds as the product)  
5. Pure AI-slop pattern YouTube is actively nuking in that vertical (identical voice + identical stock every 3 seconds + no research value) — if you can’t articulate the **value add**, kill

## Why this works (in plain English)

Your normal YouTube account already decided you are a 20–35-year-old who likes whatever you already watch. When you “research niches” on that account, YouTube shows you a **filtered internet**. The profitable older-demo sectors stay hidden.

Fix: abandon the main account. Train a blank profile to look like a **high-net-worth 55–65 American homeowner**. After ~3 days of disciplined watching, the homepage becomes a **map** of quiet channels printing money in unsaturated corners.

Teaching why older demo: significant disposable income → advertisers pay premium rates for their attention (Rick: finance examples cited in the **$10–$15** RPM conversation on tape; boring-channel teaching cites mature topics often **$8–$20** RPM range — these are **teaching ranges**, not your guarantee).

## How to costume — exact steps

### Before Day 1
1. Open **incognito**, **or** create a brand-new Google account + YouTube channel.  
2. Do **not** log into your normal YouTube. Do **not** use the TV app on your real profile.  
3. Phone away. You must actually let videos play.  
4. Have this seed list open beside you.

### Every session (~30 minutes — then stop)
1. Search the next unused seed.  
2. Open a **calm** video (history / hobby / adult money / legal / nature). Skip loud hosts, vlogs, challenge formats, talking-head gurus. Prefer **faceless** so you train toward niches you can replicate.  
3. Watch **10 full minutes**. No 2× skip-binge. Mute OK.  
4. **Like.**  
5. **Subscribe** (optional but helpful).  
6. Use “People also watched” / Suggested to open **another faceless** neighbor — still 10 minutes.  
7. Do **3 videos** total (~30 min).  
8. Close the window. Do not binge.

### Day-by-day

| Day | Job |

# Keep Going vs Pivot — Decision Tree

**What it is:** Concrete thresholds for when to persist vs kill a niche — so you don’t burn a year like Kevis did on self-improvement (monetized, ~$2 RPM, ~$1,000 in six months, first payday ~$3).

**When to use it:** After video 7, video 14, first monetization check, or any week you feel “maybe I’m the problem.”

**Doctrine:** YouTube is a consistency + ideation game. Outliers exist. So do slow channels. The tree separates **execution problems** (fixable) from **niche / RPM problems** (pivot).

---

## How to load this (1 page)

### Claude
`I’m on video __ in niche __. Here’s Studio: impressions __, views __, RPM __, upload streak __. Walk the HeadStart pivot tree. Do not invent numbers.`

### Notion
Board: Persist | Fix packaging | Park | Pivot — with threshold properties.

---

## Hard truths first

1. **Monetized ≠ good business.** Kevis’s first channel proved it.  
2. **1,000 views here and there can feel like progress and still be a trap.**  
3. **Vanity niches with teen RPMs waste the only scarce resource: nights after work.**  
4. **One viral then silence** is usually a **format** problem, not always a niche kill — see WWKD.  
5. **Impressions matter before vanity views.** Teaching rule of thumb from HeadStart brief: after ~**7 posts**, ~**1k impressions** ≈ park signal; ~**10k impressions** ≈ “alive.” Re-check on *your* niche comps — not gospel, but better than vibes.

## The tree

### Node 0 — Are you actually posting?
- **< 7 long-form uploads** → **PERSIST on process.** Fix Owner OS (ideas → script → $30 editor → thumb → upload). Do not pivot yet.  
- **≥ 7 uploads** → Node 1.

### Node 1 — Impression jail vs market response
Look at typical impressions after 48–72h on recent uploads:

| Signal | Meaning | Action |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Near-zero impressions repeatedly | Account / trust / packaging not entering auction | Fix: trusted account hygiene, title/thumb tests, don’t burn niche yet (**PERSIST + packaging**) |
| ~1k impressions after ~7 posts, flat | Weak distribution | **PARK** niche candidate; costume deeper or swap pattern |
| Growing impressions, low CTR | Packaging problem | **PERSIST** — iterate thumbs/titles (high leverage) |
| Healthy CTR, dying AVD | Script / format / promise mismatch | **PERSIST** — winning-format work, not niche kill |
| Healthy funnel, views OK, **RPM terrible** after YPP | Wrong demographic / vertical | **PIVOT** (this is the self-improvement lesson) |

### Node 2 — RPM gate (after monetization OR clear comps)

| RPM reality | Action |
|-------------|--------|
| Teaching-trap zone (~**$1–$3**) on mature effort | **PIVOT** — do not “scale a broken unit economy” |
| Mid (~**$4–$7**) with older demo unclear | Re-costume audience; check Suggested neighbors; **BORDERLINE** |
| Strong for your vertical (teaching often cites mature **~$8–$20**, finance higher) | **PERSIST** — scale format engine |

No Studio yet? Use scorecard A1–A3 + comps. Unknown → don’t assume high RPM.

### Node 3 — Banger-to-flop ratio
Over last 10 uploads:

| Pattern | Action |
|---------|--------|
| 0 standouts, all flat | Check Node 1; if impressions exist → format research (bonus: Viral Format Finder later) |
| 1 viral, 9 dead | **PERSIST niche** — extract winning format; do not chase new niches weekly |
| 2–3 repeatable standouts | **PERSIST** — you have a format engine; hire for speed |
| Declining after early luck | Audience shift or packaging decay — diagnose before pivot |

### Node 4 — Time / second-job tax
| Your week | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| >4 hours/night editing or micromanaging | You built a job — outsource edit; keep judgment only |
| ≤15–30 min/day on thumbs + upload + light QA | Owner OS healthy — judge niche on data, not fatigue |
| Inconsistent uploads (<3/week when plan was daily/near-daily) | Fix ops before blaming niche |

### Node 5 — Calendar stop-loss
| Investment | Rule |
|------------|------|
| **14 clean uploads** in a PASS-scorecard niche with impressions still dead after packaging tests | **PARK / PIVOT** |
| **30–45 days** monetized with trap RPM | **PIVOT** (don’t wait 6–12 months) |
| **90 days** daily posting, mid results, good RPM | **PERSIST** — compound (Rick shows both outlier boards and slow $2.8k/90d boards — both real) |

# What Would Kevis Do? — Scenario Playbook

**What it is:** Decision playbook for the messy middle — so you don’t need a 1:1 call for standard situations.

**How to use:** Find your scenario → do the steps in order → stop when the next action is scheduled. Pair with the pivot tree and scorecard.

---

## Scenario 1 — One video goes viral, then silence

**Symptoms:** Video 3 = rocket. Videos 4–8 = polite claps. Panic to “new niche.”

**What Kevis would do**
1. **Do not pivot the niche yet.** Virality proved demand; silence usually means you left the **format**.  
2. Tear down the viral video: hook promise, packaging, length, payoff timing, Suggested neighbors.  
3. Write the winning format in one paragraph (`05` planner lock).  
4. Make the **next 5** as variations of that format (new object, same psychology).  
5. Use cross-niche arbitrage: find the same viewer desire in adjacent niches; steal **structure**, not topics (Rick `1Kw2eIDH61A`).  
6. If after 10 format-faithful uploads nothing repeats → *then* open pivot tree.

**Failure mode:** Chasing a new niche every outlier (trial-and-error tax).

---

## Scenario 2 — Monetized but RPM is junk

**Symptoms:** YPP badge dopamine; revenue humiliating; ~$2 RPM energy.

**What Kevis would do**
1. Celebrate nothing. Open revenue tab.  
2. Check audience demographics / Suggested neighbors — are you still in teen land?  
3. Scorecard A1–A3. If trap zone → **PIVOT** (self-improvement lesson).  
4. Do not “upload harder” into a broken unit economy.  
5. Costume blank account 3 days toward 55–65 homeowners; pick PASS niche; new first 10.

**Failure mode:** Staying loyal to a niche because you already spent months.

---

## Scenario 3 — Slow start / 43 views / impression drip

**Symptoms:** Daily uploads, empty room energy, comparing yourself to highlight-reel students.

**What Kevis would do**
1. Separate **account trust / impressions** from **niche quality**.  
2. Confirm scorecard still PASS.  
3. Packaging sprint: 5 title/thumb variants on the same idea using outlier refs (1of10-style workflow from teaching).  
4. Keep edit cost low so you can afford spins.  
5. At ~7 posts: check impression heuristic (park vs alive).  
6. Remember: some boards are 90-day grind; some are day-3 rockets — both appear in teaching. Judge with the tree, not Instagram.

**Failure mode:** Becoming the editor to “try harder” (freelancer trap / second job).

---

## Scenario 4 — Niche doubt after watching a guru niche list

**Symptoms:** New PDF every week; shiny object; unfinished pipelines.

**What Kevis would do**
1. Close the guru tab.  
2. Re-open **your** scorecard sheet and costume homepage screenshot.  
3. Only entertain a new niche if it scores **≥20** and beats your current on A1–A3 + C1.  
4. One niche in production. Research queue is separate blank account — not your upload channel’s brain.  
5. Information trap = enemy. Building > learning.

**Failure mode:** Eternal research phase.

## Scenario 5 — Editor issues (late, flashy, expensive)

**Symptoms:** $80 quotes; MrBeast jump cuts; missed deadlines; you start editing “just this once.”

**What Kevis would do**
1. Re-run **$30 test**. If niche can’t be cut for $20–$30, niche may be wrong — not “hire Hollywood.”  
2. Job post must filter: calm living-room documentary, reply with code word (see leftovers pattern).

4. You never become the backup editor for more than one emergency video.  
5. Later: Editor Hiring bonus system (paid) — templates for scale.

**Failure mode:** DIY trap — saving cash by spending months of life.
